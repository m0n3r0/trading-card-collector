## 1. Why PSA 10

- **Liquidity.** PSA is the most widely recognized grader for Pokemon. A PSA 10
  sells faster and with tighter bid/ask spreads than the same card in any other
  holder, because buyers trust the grade without re-inspecting the card.
- **Price discovery.** Thousands of PSA 10 sales are recorded publicly, so fair
  value is knowable. Obscure holders have thin comp data and price opaquely.
- **Downside clarity.** If you overpay for a PSA 10, sold listings tell you
  exactly by how much. That feedback loop does not exist for ungraded cards.
- **The premium is the product.** A PSA 10 routinely sells for 2-10x the raw
  card. You are buying condition certification and resale liquidity, not just
  cardboard. Never pay the premium while thinking like a raw buyer.

## 2. What PSA 10 means, precisely

GEM-MT 10 is defined by four sub-criteria, all judged under magnification and
bright light:

| Criterion | PSA 10 tolerance |
|---|---|
| Centering | Approximately 60/40 or better, front and back |
| Corners | Sharp to the naked eye, no whitening or bends |
| Edges | Clean, no chipping, whitening, or dents |
| Surface | No scratches, print lines, stains, or indentations visible under inspection |

Additional rules that matter for Pokemon specifically:

- **No qualifiers.** PSA appends qualifiers like OC (off-center) or ST (stain)
  to lesser grades, never to 10s. A 10 is qualifier-free by definition.
- **Pack-fresh does not mean 10.** Factory print lines, edge chipping from the
  cutting process, and off-centering are common straight from sealed product,
  especially on vintage holos and modern textured cards. Most pack-fresh cards
  grade 8-9.
- **10 is not perfect.** It is the top of a tolerance band. Two 10s of the same
  card can differ visibly in centering and eye appeal. This is normal and is
  priced in for common cards; for low-population cards, premium eye appeal
  within the grade commands its own premium.
- **Misprints and errors** are graded on the same scale but trade on a separate
  market with fewer comps. Do not apply standard-card pricing to error cards.

## 3. The PSA label, cert number, and slab

- Every graded card is sealed in a tamper-evident slab with a label showing the
  card name, set, card number, grade, and a **certification number** (barcode).
- The cert number is the identity of the item. Buy by cert number: record it
  before paying, verify it on PSA's cert verification page, and confirm the
  same number arrives.
- **Label generations.** PSA has used several label designs over the years
  (paper labels, lighthouse/hologram labels, NFC-enabled labels on recent
  slabs). Match the label era to the cert age shown on verification: a 2018
  cert in a 2024-era slab is a reholder (legitimate) or a problem (illegitimate)
  depending on whether PSA's records reflect the reholder.
- **Reholder service.** PSA will re-case a card in a new slab for a fee. A
  cracked, scratched, or first-generation slab can be reholdered without
  affecting the grade. Factor $25-75 plus insured shipping each way when
  pricing ugly-slab copies.

## 4. Population reports: reading scarcity

PSA publishes population data (psacard.com/pop): how many copies of each card
have been graded at each grade.

- **Pop 10** = number of 10s. **Pop higher** = none exist above 10, by
  definition. What matters is pop 10 relative to total graded and to demand.
- **Pop growth rate** matters more than pop. A card with pop 200 that grows by
  5/month is stable; a card with pop 50 growing by 20/month after a grading
  special is about to get cheaper. Check the trend, not the snapshot.
- **Grading events distort pop.** Submission discounts and convention on-site
  grading create pop waves 2-4 months later. Prices often dip as fresh 10s hit
  the market, then recover.
- **Cross-grader supply is invisible in PSA pop.** Thousands of BGS/CGC 10s can
  cross to PSA. Low PSA pop with high combined pop elsewhere is fragile
  scarcity.
- **Set context.** Vintage print runs are fixed, so pop stabilizes. Modern sets
  are still being opened and graded, so pop climbs for years. Never pay vintage
  scarcity multiples for a modern card with growing pop.

## 5. How PSA grading works end to end

1. **Submission.** You choose a service tier by declared value per card (higher
   value = higher fee, faster turnaround). Economy tiers are cheapest with the
   longest waits; event on-site grading is fastest and most expensive.
2. **Intake and authentication.** PSA confirms the card is genuine and logs it
   to your order with a submission number you can track.
3. **Grading.** Multiple graders assess the card independently against the four
   criteria. High-value cards get additional review stages.
4. **Encapsulation.** The card is sealed, labeled, and the cert number is
   published to the population report.
5. **Return shipping.** Fully insured both ways is standard practice; budget it
   into grading economics (see section 6).

Typical all-in cost per card: $20-150 depending on tier, plus shipping and
insurance both ways. Turnarounds range from days (premium) to months (economy)
and lengthen after grading specials.

## 6. Should you grade it yourself

Grade a raw card yourself only when the math works:

```
expected_value = P(10) x price_10 + P(9) x price_9 + ... - grading_cost - shipping - time
```

- **Pre-grade honestly.** Buy a 10x loupe, check centering with a centering
  tool, and compare against PSA 9/10 exemplars. Most self-assessed "pack fresh
  10s" come back 9.
- **Grade 9 economics kill most submissions.** If price_9 barely covers
  grading cost, you need P(10) above roughly 60-70% to break even. Vintage
  holos rarely clear that bar unless the copy is exceptional.
- **Minimum quantity.** Submission minimums and flat-rate return shipping mean
  grading one card is disproportionately expensive. Batch submissions or use a
  reputable middleman service.
- **Crack and resubmit** (breaking a 9 to chase a 10) destroys value if it
  comes back 9 again or lower, and PSA tracks resubmissions. Only consider it
  when the 9-to-10 price gap exceeds roughly 5x all-in cost and the card is a
  strong 9.
- **Buy the 10 instead** when the market spread between raw-mint and PSA 10 is
  small, when you cannot pre-grade confidently, or when turnaround time matters.

## 7. How PSA 10 prices behave

- **Comps rule.** A PSA 10 is worth what the last 10-20 identical sales say,
  adjusted for trend. Everything else is storytelling.
- **The 9-to-10 cliff.** The jump from 9 to 10 is usually the largest multiple
  in the grade ladder. That cliff is steepest for iconic cards (Charizard,
  Rayquaza, Gengar, Lugia) and flattest for commons.
- **Population shocks.** Fresh pop waves depress prices for weeks to months.
  Track pop monthly on cards you hunt.
- **Set lifecycle.** New sets: hype peak in weeks 1-4 after release, decay for
  3-6 months, then slow appreciation for winners. Never buy modern PSA 10s at
  release-week prices unless the card is genuinely scarce.
- **Seasonality.** Tax-refund season and holidays lift discretionary
  collectibles; summer doldrums and major auction weeks (supply floods) soften
  them. Buy counter-seasonally when you can.
- **Icon premium.** The same pop and grade costs multiples more on a mascot
  card than on an equally scarce non-mascot. This is permanent, not a fad.
- **Macro sensitivity.** High-end slabs behave like luxury goods: they correct
  with equities and crypto. Mid-range playables are stickier. Do not stretch
  into grails on leverage, ever.

## 8. Price research: building a fair value

1. Pull the last 10-20 **sold** listings of the exact card, grade, and variant
   (eBay Sold Items filter; PSA Auction Prices Realized; PriceCharting).
2. Discard outliers (shill-bid retractions, mislabeled variants, damaged slabs
   sold as-is) and compute the median, not the mean.
3. Adjust: upward for premium eye appeal or old-cert vintage; downward for
   scratched slabs, new-cert modern with growing pop, or auction-house
   buyer's premiums you would otherwise pay (15-22%).
4. The result is your **walk-away price**. Write it down before contacting any
   seller. Every overpayment in this hobby starts with skipping this step.
5. Log comps you rely on (date, price, source URL) — the `tracker/` tool in
   this repo exists for exactly this: `tracker.py record` stores sold comps and
   `tracker.py report` compares them against your targets.

## 9. Buying online: marketplace playbook

### eBay (default for most purchases)

- Largest inventory and best price discovery. Use Sold listings to comp, Active
  listings to buy.
- Prefer sellers with 99%+ feedback across 100+ transactions. Read negative
  feedback for slab/grading complaints specifically.
- eBay's Authenticity Guarantee covers qualifying graded cards: the card routes
  through an authenticator before reaching you. Confirm eligibility at checkout.
- Offer 10-20% under ask on Buy It Now; slow-moving slabs routinely go 15-25%
  under. Bid auctions late, at your pre-set walk-away price, never above it.
- Never transact off-platform. No wire, crypto, or gift payments to strangers.

### PWCC, Goldin, Heritage (mid-range to grail)

- Curated and authenticated; strongest for vintage and $500+ cards. Weekly
  auctions often clear below eBay BIN prices.
- Always add the buyer's premium (15-22%) and shipping to your max: max hammer
  equals target price divided by 1.20 as a rule of thumb.
- Heritage for true grails, Goldin for high-end vintage/modern, PWCC
  Marketplace for fixed-price vault inventory.

### TCGplayer (modern focus)

- Fixed prices, solid buyer protection, verified-seller graded inventory. Thin
  on vintage. Good for modern PSA 10s at transparent prices.

### Facebook groups and Discord (cheapest, riskiest)

- Private sellers avoid ~13% platform fees, so prices run about 10% under eBay.
  Only for buyers who can authenticate independently.
- PayPal Goods & Services only, never Friends & Family. Demand cert number,
  front/back slab video, and seller references. Search the seller's name plus
  "scam" before paying.

### Whatnot and live sellers

- Entertainment-first; slabs move too fast for careful inspection. Only buy
  when you can freeze the frame, read the cert, and the seller has an
  established graded-card record. Screenshot everything.

### StockX and vault platforms

- Authenticated escrow-style flow but thin Pokemon selection and high prices.
  Consider only when their all-in price beats eBay-plus-risk elsewhere.

## 10. Buying offline: shops, shows, and private deals

- **Local card shops.** Expect to pay 10-20% over online comps for rent-backed
  convenience and instant inspection. Worth it for $200+ cards where fakes hurt
  most. Build a relationship: regulars get first look at new slabs and better
  trade terms.
- **Conventions and card shows.** Best offline prices through competition and
  same-day deal pressure, especially in the last two hours when dealers discount
  rather than pack inventory. Bring: phone (cert checks), loupe, printed comp
  sheet, cash in mixed bills, and a hard walk-away price per card.
- **Negotiation.** Cash is worth 3-5% off card prices to dealers (no processing
  fees, no chargeback risk). Bundling two or more slabs unlocks 10-15%. Point
  at comps, not at the ask: "last three sold at X" beats "will you take Y".
- **Private deals.** Estate and collector buyouts can price 20-40% under market
  but require authenticating every slab yourself and absorbing the risk. Verify
  each cert on the spot; photograph everything before money moves.
- **Flea markets and pawn shops.** Genuine 10s are rare; counterfeit slabs are
  common. Treat every slab as guilty until the cert verifies.

## 11. Authentication and fakes

Verify before paying, in this order:

1. **Cert lookup.** Enter the number on PSA's verification page. It must return
   this exact card, set, and grade. Nonexistent numbers and numbers resolving
   to different cards are instant walk-aways.
2. **Slab inspection.** Genuine slabs have crisp frosted edges, even sonic
   welds, sharp label printing, and a scannable barcode. Blurry labels, wrong
   fonts, missing barcodes, and rattling cards indicate counterfeit or
   compromised cases.
3. **Cert cloning.** Real number on a fake slab. Defense: match the seller's
   photos against known slab-era traits, buy from established sellers, and for
   $1,000+ insist on platform authentication or auction-house provenance.
4. **Label tampering.** 9-to-10 alterations and qualifier removals. Compare
   label stock, font weight, and hologram placement against reference slabs of
   the same era.
5. **Card swapping.** Genuine slab, wrong card inside (usually a lower grade of
   the same card). Check that the visible card matches the grade: a "10" with
   visible edge whitening is a swap.
6. **Post-purchase.** For $1,000+ from unfamiliar sellers, submit for PSA
   reholder or review: it confirms authenticity and refreshes the case.

## 12. Condition traps inside the grade

- **Weak 10s.** Bottom-of-band centering (60/40), minor print lines at the edge
  of tolerance. Priced as 10s but resell slower. For low-pop cards, pay up for
  strong eye appeal; for high-pop cards, buy the cheapest 10 and move on.
- **New-cert vs old-cert.** Old holders (early label generations) sometimes
  contain cards that would grade differently today, in both directions. Price
  the card you see, not the cert date, and be extra careful with five-figure
  old-cert slabs.
- **Scratched slabs.** Surface scratches photograph as card defects and destroy
  resale photos. Discount $25-75 for reholder cost, or demand a reholder before
  closing on expensive cards.
- **Upgraded holders.** A reholdered card keeps its grade but the fresh case
  can mask handling history. Cert history showing multiple reholders with no
  explanation deserves questions, not conclusions.

## 13. Paying, shipping, insurance, customs, taxes

- **Payment hierarchy:** platform checkout with credit card (chargeback rights)
  first, PayPal Goods & Services second. Anything else is a donation with extra
  steps.
- **Shipping standards:** tracked and insured above $100. Slab in a sleeve/team
  bag, taped between cardboard or in a slab mailer, inside a padded mailer or
  small box. Require signature above $750. **Video every unboxing** before
  touching the slab.
- **Insurance thresholds:** carrier coverage caps are low (USPS Priority covers
  $100). Buy additional declared-value coverage or use UPS/FedEx for $500+.
  Above $5,000, use auction-house vault transfer or specialty collectibles
  shippers.
- **Customs and VAT:** EU/UK buyers typically face 10-25% all-in on imports.
  Insist on accurate declarations; false values void insurance and risk
  seizure. US buyers: most single cards fall under de minimis thresholds, but
  confirm current limits before a big import.
- **Taxes:** in the US, collectibles held over a year face up to 28% federal
  capital-gains rates (plus state), distinct from securities rates; marketplace
  sales may trigger 1099-K reporting. Rules change and vary by jurisdiction:
  log every purchase (date, price, fees, seller) and consult a tax
  professional before selling at scale. This guide is not tax advice.

## 14. Storage and insurance after purchase

- Store slabs upright in slab sleeves or graded-card boxes, 35-55% humidity,
  stable room temperature, zero direct sunlight. Attics and basements are where
  collections go to die.
- Photograph every slab (front, back, cert) and keep purchase records with
  prices. This file is your insurance claim and your resale listing starter kit.
- Homeowner's policies usually cap collectibles low. Above a few thousand in
  total value, get a scheduled personal articles policy or specialty
  collectibles insurance with agreed-value coverage.
- For grails, consider auction-house vaulting: professional storage,
  instant-sell capability, and no shipping risk on resale.

## 15. Selling a PSA 10 later

- **When to sell the grade:** into strength (new pop lows are priced in,
  character anniversaries, set nostalgia waves) and before pop waves you can
  see coming. Never sell the week fresh pop hits unless you must.
- **Channel math:** eBay reaches everyone at ~13% fees; auction houses reach
  the right buyers at 15-22% but often achieve higher hammers on $1,000+ cards;
  private sale keeps ~100% but costs time and risk. Compute net, not hammer.
- **Presentation:** new slab or reholder, perfect photos (black background,
  no glare), cert number in the listing, comp-anchored starting price.
- **Taxes:** keep the purchase log from section 13. Cost basis includes
  purchase price, grading, shipping, and fees.

## 16. Checklists

### Before paying (every purchase)

- [ ] Exact variant confirmed (set, number, edition, holo pattern)
- [ ] Pop 10 and pop trend checked
- [ ] 10-20 sold comps reviewed, walk-away price written down
- [ ] Cert number verified on PSA's site, matches the card
- [ ] Slab photos inspected front and back (label, barcode, welds)
- [ ] Seller feedback and history checked
- [ ] Protected payment method selected, nothing off-platform
- [ ] Tracked + insured shipping agreed, signature threshold set

### In person (shops and shows)

- [ ] Phone charged (cert checks), loupe packed, comp sheet printed
- [ ] Cash in mixed bills plus a card backup
- [ ] Every cert verified on the spot before money moves
- [ ] Slab integrity checked in hand (welds, rattle, label lift)
- [ ] Receipt with cert numbers written on it

### On arrival

- [ ] Video the unboxing uncut
- [ ] Cert matches purchase record
- [ ] Slab intact, no new cracks or label lift
- [ ] Card inside matches grade at a glance
- [ ] Photos and records filed; issue window calendared (report problems fast)

## 17. Glossary

- **Slab:** the sealed graded-card case.
- **Cert (number):** PSA's unique identifier for a graded card.
- **Pop (population):** count of graded copies at a grade.
- **Comp (comparable):** a past sale of the identical item used to value yours.
- **Reholder:** re-casing a graded card in a new slab, grade unchanged.
- **Crack (out):** breaking a slab to free the card, usually to resubmit.
- **Qualifier:** a defect tag (OC, ST) appended to grades below 10.
- **Buyer's premium:** auction-house surcharge on the hammer price.
- **Hammer price:** the winning bid before premiums and fees.
- **Eye appeal:** subjective visual quality within a grade.
