## 2. Market lessons that transfer to PSA 10 Pokemon

1. Grading is the value engine. Pop reports plus 30 years of graded data are what
   made scarcity measurable ("only nine Mantle cards in mint or gem mint"), and
   that drove demand for the rare ones (1:00). Same logic as guide section 4.
2. Subjective grading. Same card, one grade apart, can be worth 5-10x. Budget for
   the downside, not only the 10.
3. Boom and flipping. 2020-2021 saw retail flippers, Target line fights,
   record auctions and rapid doubling in sales. A cautious commentator says things
   "don't go straight up forever" and he'd wait 2 years before entering (1:15).
   Opposing view from industry insiders: no crash unless many holders want out;
   the lesson of the 80s/90s "junk wax" overprint is being managed (1:16-1:17).
4. Collector vs. investor. Every speaker says the line is blurred. A collector's
   rule: buy what you would be happy to keep (0:03-0:04, 0:27, 0:55).
5. Supply transparency. In the junk wax era "nobody had any transparency into
   supply"; population reports plus public sold comps are what fixed that for
   the graded market (0:13). Our tracker keeps your own comps for the same reason.
6. Fakes. Resealed boxes and counterfeits exist, even for very rich buyers
   (0:08). Buy sealed vintage only from trusted sources.
7. Platforms named: eBay (Bob Means, director of trading cards), PWCC (auctions,
   vault, lending), Whatnot (live sales), Fanatics Collectibles (Topps, StockX
   founder Josh Luber), local card shops, breakers.

## 4. What to do with this in the tracker

- Log your own sold comps and sightings for any chase card (Charizard 1st Ed,
  Neo Genesis Lugia, Neo Revelation Shining cards) with source and date; the film
  shows how fast prices move in a boom.

  price; the 9-to-10 spread decides whether grading is worth the fee.
